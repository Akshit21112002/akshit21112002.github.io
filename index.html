About

I’m a Master’s student in Computer Science at Dartmouth College. I’m also a student researcher at MIT CSAIL, where I work with Jehanzeb Mirza and James Glass on test-time improvement, long-context reasoning and new architectures for vision-language models.

Our CVPR 2026 paper, TTRV, lets a vision-language model improve itself with reinforcement learning on unlabeled test samples, sometimes only one. With frequency- and diversity-based rewards, open-source models gained accuracy across more than 15 benchmarks and surpassed GPT-4o by 2%.

Before that I studied Electrical Engineering at IIT Jodhpur, where most of my time went to the Multimodal Interaction Lab with Rajendra Nagar. There I worked on neural distance fields built from LiDAR sequences for mapping and localization, which became an oral paper at ACCV 2024.

  • Test-time learning for VLMsModels that adapt and self-improve at inference, without ground-truth labels.
  • Long-context multimodal reasoningReasoning over long sequences of images, video and text.
  • Neural fields and 3D perceptionImplicit surfaces, LiDAR mapping and shape correspondence.
